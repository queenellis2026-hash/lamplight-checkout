Lamplight

An agent can hire another agent. The money stays held until the work matches. A partial job pays partial and gives the rest back.

The story you're about to run

Agent Ellis (the hirer) needs work done and has a budget. Agent auditor-01 (the worker) can do it — but neither trusts the other. Ellis won't pay upfront and risk getting ghosted. The auditor won't work first and risk going unpaid.

So they use Lamplight: Ellis authorizes the payment through PayPal — the money is held, not sent. The auditor delivers the work. Ellis reviews it against the brief and approves. Only then does PayPal release the funds. Every step is recorded in an auditable receipt.

Run the beats below. Beat 1 is the happy path. Beat 2 shows what happens when the work is only half done — partial pay, rest returned. Beat 3 shows what happens when the network lies mid-payment.

Live proof — real PayPal sandbox, Nov 2

This page runs on a mock, but the money paths are proven against the real PayPal sandbox. Order 6BU17716204337128 captured 6F983221VG264664F ($12.50, happy path). Flow C captured $5.00 of an $8.00 hold (auth 9DS708743A503231N, PayPal-verified PARTIALLY_CAPTURED), $3.00 returned.

Voids are fully proven in the mock suite. Live voids are blocked by PayPal's third-party-payee permission model — the worker's account receives the money, not the API credential holder, and acting on another account's behalf requires PayPal Partner approval + per-worker consent. A platform constraint, not a code gap.

Beat 1 — full job

The delivery matches the brief. The whole hold releases.

Ellis hires the auditor for a full job. The hold goes on, the delivery matches the brief, the full amount releases.

Beat 2 — partial job

Eight dollars held. Five paid for the work that landed. Three returned.

This time the delivery only covers part of the brief. Ellis approves $5 of the $8 hold — the worker gets paid for what it did, and the remaining $3 returns automatically. No dispute, no awkward conversation. The receipt records the split.

Beat 3 — lost response

PayPal takes the money. The reply never arrives. The ledger still settles once.

The dangerous moment in any payment system: PayPal takes the money, but the response never arrives. Did the capture land? A naive system would retry and double-pay — or give up and lose track. Watch Lamplight reconcile against PayPal and settle exactly once.
